Which video size should you use? 16:9, 9:16, 4:5 or 1:1

Updated 23 November 2026 · 2 min read

The same property film can look great on one platform and awkward on another, just because of its shape. A landscape video posted as a Reel leaves most of the phone screen black; a vertical video on YouTube sits in a narrow strip. Here is a quick way to pick the right shape.

The four shapes you need

ShapeCommon sizeBest for
16:9 landscape1920 × 1080YouTube, your website, property portals, TVs and presentations
9:16 vertical1080 × 1920Instagram and Facebook Reels, YouTube Shorts, Stories and WhatsApp Status
4:5 portrait1080 × 1350Instagram and Facebook feed posts
1:1 square1080 × 1080Feed posts and ads that appear in many places

ESTAMOTION also offers a 21:9 cinema shape – an extra-wide, film-like frame that works well as a banner video on a website or for a premium project, and an auto option that matches the shape of your photos.

Think about where people will watch

Most social viewing happens on phones held upright, so vertical 9:16 is the best default for Reels, Shorts and Status updates. People who watch on YouTube, on a laptop or on a portal’s listing page usually see a landscape player, so 16:9 fills it best. Feed posts sit in between: 4:5 takes up more of the screen than square without being cropped.

How landscape photos become a vertical video

Most listing photos are horizontal. In a vertical video, each frame shows a tall slice of the photo, and the camera pans across to reveal the rest of the room. That works well as long as the important part of each photo – the sofa, the bed, the view – is near the middle. Photos with the key feature at the very edge are better used in a landscape film.

One film per platform

If a listing matters, make two versions: a 9:16 film for Reels, Shorts and WhatsApp, and a 16:9 film for YouTube, your website and portals. It costs a few extra minutes and each platform shows the video at its best. If you can only make one, pick the shape for the place where most of your enquiries come from.

Quick checklist before you post

  • Is the shape right for this platform?
  • Is the text big enough to read on a phone, and away from the very top and bottom where buttons and captions cover it?
  • Is the video Full HD (1080 pixels on the short side)?
  • Does the first second show something worth stopping for?
