How to make a property video from listing photos

Updated 23 November 2026 · 4 min read

You don’t need a videographer or a gimbal to give a listing a video. If you have a good set of listing photos, you already have everything a short, polished property film needs. This guide walks through the whole process, from picking photos to sharing the finished MP4.

Why turn photos into a video?

Buyers and tenants scroll quickly. A photo gallery asks them to tap through image after image; a video plays on its own and walks them through the home in the order they would see it on a visit. Video also travels better: it can be posted as a Reel, shared on WhatsApp, uploaded to YouTube and added to most property portals, all from the same set of photos.

What you need before you start

  • 12–20 good photos. Sharp, well lit and taken in landscape (horizontal) if possible.
  • The key details. Locality or address, price or rent, size and configuration (for example 3 BHK, 1,450 sq ft).
  • Your contact details. Name, phone number and agency for the end card – and your RERA registration number where your state requires it in ads.
  • Music you are allowed to use. See our guide to music without copyright trouble.

Step 1: Pick the photos

Start with more photos than you need and cut down. Keep the sharpest and brightest version of each room and drop near-duplicates, blurry shots, floor plans, logos and photos with people in them. One photo per room is usually enough; give a second shot only to the rooms that sell the home, such as a large living room or a balcony with a view.

A 40–60 second film holds attention far better than a two-minute one. At about 3–4 seconds per photo, that is roughly 12–18 photos.

Step 2: Put them in the order of a visit

Viewers follow a video best when it moves through the home the way they would walk through it:

  1. Building or exterior, then the entrance
  2. Living room, then dining and kitchen
  3. Bedrooms, starting with the main bedroom
  4. Bathrooms (one or two is enough)
  5. Balcony, terrace or view
  6. Amenities such as the pool, gym, garden or clubhouse
  7. A strong final image – often the best exterior or view shot

Step 3: Choose the shape

Decide where the video will be watched first. Vertical 9:16 fills a phone screen and suits Reels, Shorts and WhatsApp Status. Landscape 16:9 suits YouTube, websites and most portals. If you are unsure, our guide to video sizes explains which shape to use where.

Step 4: Add gentle camera movement

Still photos come alive when the “camera” moves slowly across them. Use a slow push-in for rooms, a sideways pan for wide living areas and views, and a tilt for tall subjects like staircases, towers and double-height ceilings. Keep the movement slow and smooth – fast zooms feel cheap and can make viewers dizzy. Join the shots with short, soft transitions rather than flashy effects.

Step 5: Add titles and an end card

Open with a short title: the locality and the price, or the configuration and the project name. Keep it to one or two lines so it can be read in two seconds. End with a card showing your name, phone number and agency for at least three seconds, so viewers have time to note it down or take a screenshot.

Step 6: Add music

Choose a calm track that fits the property – piano or strings for a premium apartment, warm acoustic music for a family home – and keep the volume low. Fade it out over the end card. Only use music you have the right to use.

Step 7: Export and share

Export a Full-HD MP4. Upload the file directly to each platform instead of posting a link – native videos play automatically and often reach more people. In the caption, repeat the essentials: locality, configuration, price and how to contact you.

Doing it with ESTAMOTION

  1. Paste the listing link and press Get Images, or upload your photos.
  2. Tick the photos you want and drag them into the order of a visit.
  3. Check the address, price and agent details.
  4. Pick the format, transitions, colour grade, camera motion and seconds per shot.
  5. Choose the built-in music, your own track or no music, then make the film and download the MP4.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using every photo from the listing – the video drags and viewers leave.
  • Mixing day and night shots, which makes the home feel disjointed.
  • Crooked photos – tilted walls look worse once the camera starts moving.
  • Tiny text that can’t be read on a phone.
  • Posting a landscape video as a Reel, leaving most of the phone screen empty.
