Music for property videos without copyright trouble

Updated 23 November 2026 · 3 min read

Music sets the mood of a property film in the first few seconds. But the wrong track can get your video muted, blocked or removed – and a property video is advertising, so the rules are stricter than for a personal post. Here is how to choose music safely.

Why popular songs cause problems

Film songs and chart hits are owned by music labels and publishers. Platforms like YouTube, Instagram and Facebook scan uploads for this music automatically. When they find it, the video may be muted, blocked in some countries, taken down, or the owner may claim the video. Downloading a song or buying it for personal listening does not give you the right to use it in an advertisement.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. When in doubt, read the licence for the track or ask the music provider.

Safe places to get music

  • Royalty-free music libraries. Paid libraries sell licences that cover use in videos, often including commercial use. Check that your plan covers advertising and the platforms you post on.
  • YouTube Audio Library. Inside YouTube Studio there is a free library of music for YouTube videos. Some tracks require you to credit the artist in the description – the library tells you which.
  • In-app music on Instagram and Facebook. Music added inside the app is licensed for use there. Business accounts usually see a smaller selection, because many popular songs are not cleared for commercial use.
  • Music you own or commission. A local musician can compose a short signature track for your agency that you can use everywhere.
  • Built-in scores. ESTAMOTION’s built-in ambient score is generated by the app itself, so there are no royalties to pay.

Keep proof of your licence

Save the receipt, licence certificate or a screenshot of the track’s licence page. If a video is ever claimed by mistake, this is what you send to the platform to dispute it.

Choosing a track that fits

  • Premium apartments and villas: slow piano, strings or ambient music.
  • Family homes: warm acoustic guitar or soft, uplifting tracks.
  • Commercial spaces and new launches: modern electronic music with a steady beat.
  • Rentals for young professionals: light, upbeat lo-fi or pop instrumentals.

Prefer instrumental music – lyrics compete with your on-screen text. Keep the volume low enough that a voice-over would still be clear, and let the music fade out over the end card.

Match the edit to the music

Shots that change on the beat feel more professional. With 3–4 seconds per photo, a slow or medium tempo usually fits best; fast music with slow camera moves feels rushed and mismatched. If your film is 45 seconds, pick a track that is at least that long, or one designed to loop, so it doesn’t stop abruptly.

No music at all?

Silence is better than a risky track. Many viewers watch with the sound off anyway, so strong on-screen text matters more than the soundtrack. You can always add licensed in-app music when you post on Instagram.
