How to shoot property photos with your phone

Updated 23 November 2026 · 3 min read

A modern phone can take listing photos that look professional, as long as you get the basics right. Good photos make the listing look better and they make listing videos look better too, because the camera moves zoom into each photo and show up every flaw.

Prepare the home first

  • Clear kitchen counters, tables and bathroom shelves.
  • Remove personal items: family photos, documents, toothbrushes, shoes at the door.
  • Make the beds, straighten cushions and curtains, and hide cables where you can.
  • Close toilet lids and switch off ceiling fans so the blades don’t blur.

Use as much daylight as you can

Open every curtain and blind and switch on all the lights. Shoot during the day, ideally mid-morning or mid-afternoon, when light is bright but not harsh. Avoid mixing photos taken at different times of day – a set where some rooms are sunny and others are dark feels inconsistent, especially in a video.

Turn on HDR (most phones do this automatically). It keeps detail both in the bright window and in the darker room.

Hold the phone horizontally and level

Take photos in landscape. A horizontal photo has room for a camera move in a landscape video and can still be cropped for a vertical Reel; a vertical photo can’t be widened later. Turn on the grid lines in your camera settings and keep vertical lines – door frames, wall corners, windows – straight up and down. Tilted walls are the most common sign of an amateur photo.

Shoot from chest height and from the corners

Hold the phone at about chest height, not above your head and not at waist level. Stand in a doorway or a corner of the room and aim across it, so the photo shows the room’s size and how the space connects. Take two or three angles of every room so you have a choice later.

Use the wide lens carefully

The ultra-wide (0.5×) lens is useful in small bedrooms and bathrooms, but it stretches furniture at the edges of the frame. Step back with the normal lens when you can, and use the wide lens only when the room really needs it. Never use digital zoom – walk closer instead.

Tap to focus, and clean the lens

Tap the screen on the middle of the room to set focus and brightness, and slide the brightness down slightly if the windows turn pure white. Wipe the lens before you start – a fingerprint smear is the most common cause of hazy, soft photos.

Don’t forget the outside and the details

  • The building, the entrance and the gate or lobby
  • The view from the balcony or main windows
  • Amenities: pool, gym, garden, play area, clubhouse, parking
  • A few close-ups of quality details: fittings, flooring, modular kitchen

Avoid mirrors that reflect you, and wait for people and vehicles to move out of the frame.

Share photos without losing quality

Messaging apps often compress photos to save data. When you send photos to a colleague or upload them for a video, send the original files – for example as a document, with the app’s HD option, or through a cloud drive link. For video, aim for photos at least 1,600 pixels wide; full-resolution phone photos are ideal.
